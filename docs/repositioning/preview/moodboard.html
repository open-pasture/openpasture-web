It started with two sheep and a slope.

Cody bought two sheep to clear an overgrown hillside behind his house. There was no perimeter fence, so he tried electric netting. The incline made it hard to set, moving it took too long, and grounding never quite worked. The bottleneck was never the sheep. It was one person's ability to move them.

He wanted a collar he could buy as a tool and write software against. He couldn't find one on terms he liked, so he's building it, alongside his job and his family.

The collar acts in the pasture. The intelligence layer decides what should happen.

The collar is a physical tool with open interfaces. Draw a boundary on a map, and the collar holds the animals inside it. Replace the battery. Assemble one from a kit. Connect software that isn't ours. That is the first thing being built, and the only thing being built right now.

The intelligence layer comes later. It takes farm context, grazing knowledge, collar telemetry, satellite and weather data, and your field notes, runs them through a model you choose, and proposes the next move. You approve every move at first. Automation, if you want it, comes after that.

Three questions the app should answer.

How is the farm doing. Where and when is the next move. What needs your attention. When automation is on, a planned move shows a countdown, and you can hold it, pick a preset, or redraw the boundary before it runs.

This is a concept of the screen, not a live product. The values are made up.

What comes next, in order.

  • A working collar and a map you can draw boundaries onnow
  • Software operating the collar through open interfaces
  • Proposed moves you approve one at a time
  • Scheduled moves you can hold or change before they run
  • A working sheep farm to test the agriculture and the economics
  • More frequent moves, more species, shared pasture

Ownership is the point.

We plan to sell components, kits, assembled collars, and an optional software subscription. Bundles that make the hardware cheaper are fine. What doesn't change is that the collar stays yours. You can repair it, run other software on it, and where the hardware supports it, bring your own connectivity. If a farmer builds a hundred of them, or a shop sells them, that's the design working.

The mission is to move animal agriculture onto pasture. The way to prove it is to run a farm on this and publish what happens.
