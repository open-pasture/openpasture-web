Roadmap

Sheep, then cattle, then poultry

Two orderings run at once. One is which animal we serve. The other is how far down the stack we go. We are deliberately slow on the second: every layer of hardware is only worth building once the decision layer above it has proven it knows what to ask for.

By species

Where the animals lead

NOW — SHEEP

Research and first customers

Sheep move readily, tolerate dense rotation, and run through a paddock cycle fast enough that we learn in weeks rather than seasons. That makes them the right place to prove the grazing model and the right first market. We would rather be genuinely useful to sheep farmers than plausibly useful to everyone.

NEXT — CATTLE

Where the volume is

Cattle account for the bulk of grazed land and the bulk of meat eaten in the United States, so this is where the business actually lives. Nothing about the model changes: same farm objects, same planner, same daily question. Larger paddocks, longer recovery windows, higher stakes per decision.

LATER — POULTRY

Automated coops

Pastured poultry follows ruminants across the same ground and gets the most out of what they leave behind. It is also the clearest case for crossing into hardware: a coop that advances itself on the schedule the planner already computes is a far smaller problem than steering a herd.

By layer

Software first, telemetry second, hardware third

PHASE 01 — SHIPPING

The decision layer

Connect the agents farmers already use to farm state, grazing knowledge, and telemetry. The daily loop ships as a recommendation — move, stay, or needs-info — executed by hand. This proves the decision layer is worth trusting before anything automatic depends on it.

PHASE 02

The sensing layer

Productionize ingestion: Planet pipelines out of prototype, drone and PIX4D orthomosaics, pasture sensors and cameras, and integrations with existing collar vendors wherever their systems allow it. The plan gets sharper without the farmer typing more.

PHASE 03

The hardware layer

Automated coops for poultry, and open, repairable collars for ruminants. Owned outright, serviceable by the farmer, and useless to us as a lock-in mechanism because the software that drives them is AGPL. Design work only today. Nothing to buy.

THE FAR END

The standard, met

Fresh grass every 24 hours, at a labor cost low enough that doing it right is cheaper than doing it wrong. At that point the argument stops being ours to make.
